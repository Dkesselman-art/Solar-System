Our solar system has five officially recognized dwarf planets: Ceres, Pluto, Haumea, Makemake, and Eris. Like planets, they orbit the Sun and are massive enough to pull themselves into a nearly round shape, but unlike planets, they have not cleared their orbits of other objects. Ceres is the closest, sitting in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter. The other four lie in the cold, distant reaches beyond Neptune. Pluto is the largest dwarf planet by size, while Eris is the most massive, and its discovery in 2005 led to Pluto being reclassified from planet to dwarf planet in 2006.
