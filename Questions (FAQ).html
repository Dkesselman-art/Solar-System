In 2006, the International Astronomical Union decided a planet must orbit the Sun, be big enough to pull itself into a round shape, and clear its orbit of other objects. Pluto shares its orbit with many icy objects in the Kuiper Belt, so it was reclassified as a dwarf planet.

Venus is the hottest planet, at about 870°F (465°C), even though Mercury is closer to the Sun. Venus's thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat like a greenhouse.

Jupiter is the largest planet. It has more than twice the mass of all the other planets combined, and about 1,300 Earths could fit inside it.

Ganymede, one of Jupiter's moons, is the largest. It is even bigger than the planet Mercury.

All four giant planets have rings: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. Saturn's rings are by far the brightest and easiest to see.
